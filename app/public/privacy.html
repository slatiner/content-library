Privacy policy

Last updated 19 October 2026. This is a small, invite-only, open-source hobby project.

This app keeps a personal log of the books, films, series and music you've read, watched or listened to. This page says what it stores, who can see it, and what leaves the app. There are no ads, no analytics and no tracking cookies.

What's stored

  • Your account: your email address, name and profile picture link from Google sign-in, and when you joined.
  • Your library: every entry you add (title, people, dates, rating, cover link) and your notes. Notes can mention other people, so keep that in mind when writing them.
  • Your settings: your library's default visibility, the share links you've made, and (for admins) the invite codes you've created.

The browser also keeps your sign-in session, and a pending invite code while you sign in, in its local storage. The database also counts your assistant requests for the current day. Nothing else is stored about you.

Where it's stored

In a Supabase database in London (AWS eu-west-2). Google handles sign-in. The site itself is served by GitHub Pages. An encrypted backup of the database is made weekly and kept for 90 days, readable only by the app's owner.

Who can see it

  • Private entries: only you.
  • Friends entries: only you, and your friends once friendships exist.
  • Public entries: other members, and anyone you give a share link to.
  • Notes are never shared, whatever the entry's level.

New accounts start private. You can change the default and each entry's level under Sharing, and revoke a share link there at any time. The app's owner can technically access the database to run and repair it, and doesn't read your library otherwise.

What leaves the app

  • The assistant: text you type into "Describe it" or into a plain-language search is sent to Groq to be understood. Only what you type is sent, not your library or notes. Groq processes it in the US, doesn't use it for training, and keeps no copy (zero data retention is switched on). Each member can make 50 assistant requests a day.
  • Lookups and covers: when you look up a title, its name is sent to Wikipedia/Wikidata, Open Library, TVmaze or MusicBrainz. Cover images load from those sites, so they see your IP address, like any image on the web.

Nothing is sold or shared with anyone else.

Your choices

  • Download your data: account menu → Your data → Download my data gives one JSON file with everything above, notes included.
  • Delete your account: account menu → Your data → Delete my account removes your account, library and share links right away. Copies age out of the backups within 90 days.
  • Accounts that sign in but never redeem an invite are deleted automatically after about a month.

Credits

Details and covers are looked up from these free sources, and the covers are loaded straight from their sites. Thank you to all of them.

  • Wikidata: titles, years and people (CC0).
  • Wikipedia: film and series posters. These are non-free images that Wikipedia uses under fair use, shown here only to identify the work. Wikimedia Commons: public-domain covers only.
  • TVmaze: series posters (TVmaze API, CC BY-SA).
  • Open Library: book covers and work ids.
  • MusicBrainz and the Cover Art Archive: albums and album covers.

Covers remain the property of their rights holders. If you hold the rights to one and want it removed, open an issue (below).

Questions

Open an issue at github.com/slatiner/content-library/issues. Issues are public, so don't put personal details in them.
